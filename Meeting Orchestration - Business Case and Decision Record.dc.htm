1 · Purpose and scope

This document records why the bank chose to build a meeting-orchestration application for Relationship Managers, what alternatives were considered and rejected, and what the initiative commits to deliver and measure. It exists so that the reasoning survives the people who made it: anyone joining the workstream, reviewing it at audit, or reopening the question in two years should be able to read this alone and understand the decision.

It is written for the Head of Customer Journeys and Head of Banking as accountable owners, for COO and operations leadership as the sponsors of the process change, for Risk and Compliance as evidence that the control implications were considered before build, and for the general reader who finds it on SharePoint.

In scope of this document

The end-to-end journey of arranging a client meeting: from the customer's request through to a confirmed calendar entry with room, catering and reception arranged. Technology choice, delivery approach, benefits and the control posture at business level.

Out of scope

Technical design and configuration detail (see Document B). Meeting content, client advice or any part of the advisory process. Changes to Dynamics 365 as a system of record. Lunches, intermediary meetings and complex multi-party events, which remain with Team Assistants.

2 · Executive summary

Every fact needed to arrange a client meeting already exists in a Microsoft system the bank owns. The client record is in Dynamics 365, internal availability is in Outlook, rooms are bookable, and a partner's availability is obtainable from an Executive Assistant. None of it is joined. The Relationship Manager is the integration layer — checking four places, creating provisional invites that are mostly deleted, and running a mail thread of six to eight messages to agree one date.

The bank has endorsed building a Power Platform code app that presents this journey on one screen: the RM enters or pastes the request, the app resolves internal availability, rooms and the partner's diary, a Copilot Studio agent reads the request and drafts the invitation, the RM reviews and sends, and the customer picks a time in one tap. Fulfilment — calendar event, room, catering, reception, audit record — happens automatically below the line of visibility.

Nothing leaves the Power Platform. Same tenancy, same connectors, same DLP policy, same audit trail and the same ALM pipeline the bank already approved. No separate AI service is provisioned, key-managed or billed. The AI reads and drafts; it does not decide and it does not send.

What was decided Detail
Build patternPower Platform code app (React inside the platform) — not a canvas app, model-driven app, or Microsoft Bookings.
AI componentCopilot Studio agent, called as a connector. Supersedes an earlier proposal to call Azure OpenAI directly.
Human controlTwo mandatory review gates. The RM confirms extracted details and approves every outbound message.
Delivery routeExisting Power Platform environments and ALM pipeline; pilot with a small group of RMs before wider rollout.
Benefits basisTwo measured numbers, baselined before the pilot and re-measured after. No modelled business case.

3 · The problem

The data is not missing. It is not joined. Three characteristics of the current arrangement make it expensive.

Many relationships, no shared memory

One RM holds a book of clients, each with a different partner, sensitivity and meeting rhythm. Nothing in the booking flow knows any of that, so the RM re-supplies it every time.

Four places to look

The client record in Dynamics, three or four internal diaries in Outlook, a room, and — where a partner is involved — availability that arrives by email from an Executive Assistant. The RM reconciles these by hand.

No streamlined experience

Provisional invites are created and then deleted; dates are chased by email. What the client experiences depends on who is arranging it. There is no record of what was proposed, chosen and confirmed beyond the mail thread.

6 · Options considered

Four options were assessed against the same criterion: what happens to the RM's journey. Doing nothing was assessed first.

Option Effect on the RM journey Speed to change Reuse elsewhere
Do nothing
Bookings · findMeetingTimes		 Finds a time, but knows nothing about the client, the partner or the provisional holds. The RM still joins it up. n/a None to build on
Canvas app
Low-code, drag and drop		 Fine for a form. This flow has multi-step state, drafts and review gates — the point at which canvas becomes hard to hold together. Fast at first, slow later Copy and paste screens
Model-driven app
Dataverse-first		 Strong for caseworking on records. The RM journey is a guided flow, not a form over a table. Config-led Tied to its tables
Code app CHOSEN
React in Power Platform		 The journey is designed once, in full: real interaction, real state, one screen the RM never leaves. Same-day change Shell reusable by other journeys

Nothing leaves the platform. The code app runs in the same tenancy, uses the same connectors, and is governed by the same DLP policy and audit trail as any other Power Platform application the bank runs.

6.1 One correction to the original proposal

An earlier version of the proposal called Azure OpenAI directly. That was changed to a Copilot Studio agent before endorsement. The agent has two jobs — read the customer's email, draft the invitation — and makes no decisions and no sends. Three consequences follow:

  • Nothing new to stand up. No AI service to provision, key-manage or bill separately; one connector call from the app.
  • Inside existing governance. The same tenancy, DLP policy and audit trail the bank already approved for Power Platform.
  • Changed without a release. Tone, topics and guardrails are maintained in Copilot Studio, not shipped as code.

Four outcomes are claimed. Two numbers will be measured; the other two are qualitative and will be evidenced by RM and client feedback during the pilot.

8 · Risks, assumptions and dependencies

Ref Risk Likelihood Impact Mitigation
R1The AI draft misreads a client request and the RM approves it without noticingMediumHighExtracted fields are visibly marked as AI-sourced, low-confidence fields flagged for checking, and the RM must confirm details before proceeding. Every draft is fully editable.
R2Client personal data is exposed to an AI service outside approved boundariesLowHighCopilot Studio within the bank's tenancy; no external AI endpoint. DLP policy enforced at connector level. Detail in Document B §8 and §10.
R3RMs continue to use the old process alongside the new oneMediumMediumPilot with a small, willing group; weekly change loop so friction is removed rather than tolerated; lead-banker view shows adoption.
R4A code app is seen as bespoke software the bank must support foreverMediumMediumSame tenancy, pipeline and support model as other Power Platform applications; source control and code review as with any bank system.

R6Partner availability still arrives out of band because EAs are outside the appMediumLowPartner diaries read through Graph where permissions allow; EA involvement retained as a documented exception path.

Assumptions

  • Client and partner relationship data in Dynamics 365 is accurate enough to drive attendee selection.
  • Graph permissions permit reading free/busy for internal attendees, including partners, at the required granularity.
  • Meeting rooms are bookable as Exchange resources across the offices in scope.
  • Existing Power Platform ALM, DLP and support arrangements extend to this application without exception.

Dependencies

  • Tenant environments and connector approvals for the pilot.
  • Copilot Studio licensing and agent authoring access.
  • Facilities and catering teams to receive automated notifications in place of email.
  • Security and Compliance review of Document B before pilot go-live.

12 · Questions raised, and the answers given

These were the questions put to the workstream before endorsement. They are recorded here because they will be asked again.

Question Answer
Why not just use Bookings?It finds a slot. It cannot read the client relationship, hold three options, or bring the partner in.
Another app to maintain?Same tenancy, same pipeline, same support model as the bank's other Power Platform applications.
Does the AI decide anything?No. It reads the email and drafts the words. The RM approves every time and every send.
What about the assistants?Lunches, intermediaries and complex meetings stay with them. The repetitive provisioning goes.
Why not wait for the roadmap?Nothing on it joins Dynamics, diaries and rooms into one RM view. That gap is the bank's to close.

Appendix A · Glossary

Term Meaning
